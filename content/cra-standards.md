Harmonised standards are how most manufacturers will show that a product meets the [Cyber Resilience Act](<https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/2847/oj/eng>) essential requirements. Once a standard is cited in the Official Journal of the EU, applying it gives a presumption of conformity with the requirements it covers (Article 27). For important products in Class I, a fully applied cited standard also opens the internal-control route (module A).

The Commission's standardisation request M/606 to CEN, CENELEC and ETSI lists 41 deliverables. Fewer standards answer them: EN 40000-1-4 covers lines 2 to 14 on its own, and a few product standards cover two lines each. The table also includes related work that is not a line in M/606, such as the EN 40000-1-1 vocabulary and the IEC 62443 amendments. Each row shows where the standard stands, grouped by the products it covers.

**No CRA harmonised standard is cited in the Official Journal yet.** Until one is, manufacturers assess against the essential requirements of Annex I directly. Most standards in the table are expected to be cited and will then give a presumption of conformity. The few tagged *No OJ citation expected* are not, even once they are approved and published.
