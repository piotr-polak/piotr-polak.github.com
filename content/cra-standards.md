Harmonised standards are how most manufacturers will show that a product meets the [Cyber Resilience Act](<https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/2847/oj/eng>) essential requirements. Once a standard is cited in the Official Journal of the EU, applying it gives a presumption of conformity with the requirements it covers (Article 27). For important products in Class I, a fully applied cited standard also opens the internal-control route (module A).

The Commission asked CEN, CENELEC and ETSI for 41 standards in standardisation request M/606. The table shows where each one stands, grouped by the products it covers.

**No CRA harmonised standard is cited in the Official Journal yet.** Until one is, manufacturers assess against the essential requirements of Annex I directly. A standard can also be approved by CEN, CENELEC or ETSI without being cited: standards tagged *OJ citation expected* are the ones intended to give presumption of conformity once cited.
